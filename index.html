Why Naseem is different

Most assistants stop at an answer. Naseem keeps going.

Inspect the project, make the change, run the tools, read the results, verify, and fix — on your real files, in your real terminal.

01

Works with your real files

Inspect, read, patch and create files on disk. No copy-pasting between a chat window and your editor.

02

Has a real terminal

git, xcodebuild, package managers, tests, scripts, Python. Your Mac's actual environment, not a browser sandbox.

03

Drives the Simulator

It sees the app's real buttons and labels and acts on them by name — not by guessing from screenshots.

04

Drives native Mac apps

The same approach through Apple's Accessibility, for GUI-only apps like Notes, Mail and System Settings.

05

Proves, doesn't assert

A "done" is a claim to check. A file that isn't on disk sends the reply back; a tap on a changed screen is refused.

06

Stays fast on long tasks

Hundreds of steps without the conversation getting heavier and slower with every one.

07

Nothing claimed early

Feedback from the forum has become releases within hours. A principle the app doesn't keep counts as a bug.

08

Conductor, not orchestra

Written entirely in Swift. It stays light while Xcode, the Simulator and the model do the heavy lifting.

Long-task engineering

Built to stay sharp over hundreds of steps.

The hard part of an agent isn't answering — it's staying useful without drowning in its own context or losing the plot.

Light on every step

Finished work doesn't pile up, so a long run stays quick and affordable.

Memory that survives restarts

Ask about yesterday's failed build and the agent pulls up the actual log.

Stays on mission

It keeps the goal in view on every step, and stops to report back instead of grinding.

Crash-safe runs

Quit mid-task and completed steps are kept; continue picks up with memory of what was done.
